# Total Engineering: proposal for the zoomable talk

How I imagine the talk as one poster with a linear camera tour. Synthesised from five investigations (narrative, closing, visual composition, technical architecture, zoomable-talk craft). The two earlier documents remain as reference material; this document supersedes their second half and their camera plan.

## 1. The one-line idea

Your specialty is your home position. The whole product is your pitch.

The talk borrows one idea from Total Football: a player can leave their position because a teammate covers, and the team keeps its shape. The claim of the talk is that with AI assistance the labels FE, BE, QA and DevOps describe where each of us started, not where we have to stay. It is an inspirational talk for engineers across teams, not a plan for one team. Nothing on stage announces a reorganisation; it invites people to believe that range is available to them.

On wording: "AI engineer" already means someone who builds AI products, so the talk does not use it for this idea. It says "full-stack" once as a translation and otherwise uses the title's own word: total engineer, or simply engineer.

## 2. What changes from the earlier draft

- Everything up to Total Engineering stays, tightened, with one addition: the pitch first shows the real 1974 line-up, and Total Football is explained on real names before the engineering labels appear.
- The invented "parked idea" story, the 60-second writing exercise, Team Shape and The Assist go. They restate Cover three times and end the talk twice.
- The honest limit moves on stage in one short beat, so that the inspiration is credible to the people most likely to resist it.
- The close returns to the opening feeling and gives one small, optional thing anyone can do this week. No team photo, no rhetorical question.
- Each place on the poster is visited once. The route is a clockwise spiral with no backtracking.

The fact check found nothing wrong in the earlier draft. Beck's post and follow-up, Michels and 1974, and the 1974 final line-up are all verified. The risk was omission, not error.

## 4. From the analogy to something motivating

The metaphor alone is a nice picture. Five moves turn it into "this is about me".

1. **Make it real before making it ours.** The 1974 line-up with real names, and the opening minute of the final, are verified history. When the same shape then carries FE, BE, QA and DevOps, the audience has already accepted that the idea worked once.
2. **Tell people what they already do.** Everyone in the room has crossed a line. Naming that turns the talk from "you should change" into "you already have, and now it is cheaper".
3. **Cross out the alibis.** The three sentences that keep people in their lane are written down and struck through with the same stroke as FOOTBALL in the title. The gesture from the opening becomes the argument.
4. **Keep one honest beat.** AI writes, it does not know when it is wrong, and judgment comes from experience. Said once, in one line, it is what makes the inspiration credible to the people most likely to resist it. It also reframes their experience as the remaining 10 percent with more leverage, which is the most motivating thing a specialist can hear.
5. **Ask for something optional and small.** One thing outside your label, this week, shown to someone who knows. No pledge, no exercise, no names.

The AI framing that survives skeptics is range, not speed: more things you can make work yourself, with someone covering. Engelbart's 1962 augmentation paper treats the tool as one of four parts alongside training, language and method. No 10x, no future of work, no promise about months from now, and no claim that specialist roles disappear.

## 5. The close, in plain words

The close is three things said in order, in your own voice, without slogans.

1. **The ask, on the notebook.** One thing outside your label this week. Ask the tool to explain it, build the rough version, show it to someone who knows that area. That is movement with cover.
2. **The callback, on the pullback.** The first time you made something work, nobody asked what your label was. That feeling is still available, and it now reaches more of the product than it used to.
3. **The last thing you say.** The one-line idea of the talk, once, in whatever words are natural to you, or nothing at all. The pitch without bands already says it.

Then stop and stay on the whole poster for questions.

Things to avoid: a closing question with no owner such as "What shall we build together?", quotable lines that sound written, any Cruyff line beyond the Foundation's verified sayings, "voetbal is oorlog" (not Michels), and the claim that anyone's role is going away.

Each close view is 3x to 4x the poster view. Every permanent object clears every other stop's frame by at least 400 pixels so nothing bleeds in at 4K.

**The pitch as a stage.** Decided on 9 September 2026: the photographic pitch.png is the ground, used as is, with a full-resolution regeneration or upscale for deep zooms. Everything that moves or reads sits on top as vector: department bands, markers, ball, trails, labels. Labels on grass carry a dark outline for projector contrast. Departments are four vertical bands in delivery order, drawn as grease pencil on a tactics board: charcoal dashed dividers and faintly tinted bands, nothing orange. Eleven numbered orange discs in a 4-3-3, white numbers, dark ring, soft shadow. Ball as a white disc that stops dead at a divider. Movement trails orange, cover trails white, AI possibilities as three dotted orange paths of which one becomes solid.

**The rest of the poster.** Decided on 9 September 2026: the target look is the second reference image (bg2.png), a scrapbook wall with the pitch clean in the centre and the objects grouped around it: 1974 photo upper left, laptop with the first creation lower left, Beck note upper right, notebook lower right, title on top. The ground is regenerated from that image without any text and without photo contents, keeping the empty frames, tape, coffee, whistle, boots and map, then upscaled. Real photos and all handwriting go in as separate layers. No slogans survive from the reference; handwritten labels carry only real content such as Movement, Cover, names and numbers. There is no team photo; the bottom centre stays scrapbook clutter, or holds the boots and the whistle as the "home position" objects.

**Real photographs.** The archive place uses a verified CC0 Anefo photo from the Nationaal Archief on Wikimedia Commons: the Netherlands squad training in Hiltrup, 13 June 1974 (item 927-2551), Keizer, Jongbloed, Van Hanegem, De Jong and Cruijff with Fadrhonc. Alternatives are the 4 July training (927-3062) and the Michels, Cruijff and Rijsbergen photo after the final. Credit line: Anefo / Nationaal Archief, CC0. None of the generated photos in bg1.png are used as archive material.

**Type.** Anton for the big titles, chapter words and player numbers. Permanent Marker, skewed slightly, for handwritten labels. Archivo for everything small: tags, the Beck quotation, captions. The master title is the one piece of artwork that stays a bitmap vectorised from title-no-bg.png, because no font matches its dry brush.

**Contrast rules from the projector research.** Orange never as text or thin lines on green, only as filled shapes. Labels white with a dark halo. Body text at close views at least 4.5% of screen height. Lines at least 3 screen pixels.

## 7. How it runs

impress.js v2.0.0, one vendored file, no build step, 2D only. Steps are transparent camera frames over one poster element. Each step and each substep carries a full description of what is visible, so going back with the clicker, reloading, or jumping to any stop always rebuilds the correct picture.

```
talk/
  index.html          poster DOM, steps, speaker notes per step
  vendor/impress.js
  css/poster.css
  js/director.js      about 200 lines: scene parser, SVG state, timer, clicker safety
  img/                paper tile, grass tile, cutouts, photos
```

- **Speaker console** with notes, current and next view, clock and elapsed time comes with impress.js. It needs a local server (`python3 -m http.server`), since Chrome blocks it from a file URL. The director adds per-chapter target times.
- **Clicker.** Logitech R400 class remotes send PageUp, PageDown, a period for blank, and F5 or Escape for the play button. The first three work out of the box. F5 and Escape get suppressed so the play button cannot drop you out of fullscreen. Next and previous are debounced for the transition duration so a double click cannot skip a stop.
- **Crispness.** Text, lines and markers are live DOM and SVG, so they re-rasterise sharp when the camera settles. Photos sit at native size with their close stop at 1:1. No 7000 pixel bitmap is shipped; paper and grass are seamless tiles.
- **Fallback.** DeckTape has an impress.js plugin that walks every step and substep, giving a PDF of every state for the venue laptop or a recording.
- **Motion.** 0.9 to 1.3 seconds between neighbours, up to 2 seconds for the whole-pitch reveal and the final pullback. Long moves zoom out, pan, zoom in. Zero rotation. Objects only move once the camera is still. Reduced-motion preference switches moves to cross-fades.

Present from Chrome. Test on the real laptop early for memory at the overview and the blur settle time, and rehearse twice with the real clicker at a 16:10 window.
