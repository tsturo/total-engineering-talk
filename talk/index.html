There's a Dutch idea behind this title. First, think back to the first thing you made work.
Can you remember the first time you made a computer do something and thought: it works? Something happened on the other side of the screen because of a decision you made. Nobody asked what your label was.
The Netherlands, 1974. Rinus Michels was the coach, Johan Cruyff the conductor on the pitch. They lost the final to West Germany, 2 to 1, and still changed how football is played. Introduce the idea in one sentence: any player could take any position, because a teammate covered.
The Netherlands, 1974 World Cup final. A real team and a real idea. Rinus Michels, Johan Cruyff. Explain the line-up briefly for people who do not follow football.
From kick-off the Dutch kept the ball for about a minute. Cruyff was fouled in the box. Neeskens scored the penalty in the second minute before any German had touched the ball. Then the idea itself. Suurbier, the right back, overlaps up the wing and gets the ball; Jansen drops in to cover his place. Neeskens, a midfielder, surges into the box and gets the next pass; Van Hanegem slides across to hold the middle. And when the ball is lost, the same eleven drop into a compact block. Nobody is told. You can go, because someone covers, and the shape never breaks. That is Total Football.
Strip it down to four. A goalkeeper. A defender. A midfielder. A striker. Four positions, four kinds of skill. This is the simplest picture of a team, and it is the picture we will put our own labels on next.
The goalkeeper. Brilliant with his hands, solid at the back, nobody expects him to score. His shape is his home position.
The defender. Strong in the tackle, reads the game, good legs, a decent pass out. He can score from a corner but nobody builds the attack around him.
The midfielder. The best passer on the pitch, runs all day, joins the attack, helps the defence. The widest shape of the four.
The striker. One job, done brilliantly. Fast, clinical, and not much use at the back. Every team needs one, and nobody asks him to keep goal.
Four positions, four profiles. Each one is excellent at something and weak at something else, and the team works because the shapes fit together. Hold this for a moment.
Now imagine one player who could do all of it. Keep goal, defend, pass, score, run all day. This is what Michels asked of every player in 1974: not to be the best at one thing, but to be able to take any position when the game needed it. That is the total footballer. Keep this shape in mind. We are about to draw it for an engineer.
Back to the four. First the lanes: DevOps, QA, Backend, Frontend. Now watch them change, one by one, with their profile. The goalkeeper becomes DevOps: nobody notices him until something is down, and he is the only one allowed to touch production with his hands. The defender becomes QA: reads the game, catches what everyone missed. The midfielder becomes Backend: the engine room. The striker becomes Frontend: the one who finishes, because the feature exists when someone can click it. Six axes: frontend, backend, testing, operations, communication, judgment. Every one a different polygon. That is a team.
Pull back and look at the whole thing. Where we started, the first program that worked. The 1974 team that changed how football is played. And our four, in their lanes, each excellent at something. The pitch is the same. Stay here for questions.
