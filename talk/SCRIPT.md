# Total Engineering: speaker script

About 30 minutes plus the live demo. One line per stop is the thought to land; the rest is what to say around it. "Click" means one press on the clicker.

The arc in one breath: you started as someone who made a thing work, not as a label. Total Football showed that specialists who can cover for each other beat any fixed shape. AI makes the first step into someone else's lane cheap, so we can play like that too. And with agents, you also become the coach who draws the shape.

---

## 0. Title (0:15)

**Thought:** the title is a Dutch idea, not a slogan.

- Struck-through Football, Engineering written over it. That is the whole talk: a football idea applied to how we build.
- Before the football, one question for the room.

## 1. It works (2:00)

**Thought:** you started before you had a label.

- Windows XP on screen. Can you remember the first time you made a computer do something and thought: it works?
- Click, Turbo Pascal. Mine looked like this. A loop, a blue screen, and something happened on the other side of the glass because of a decision I made.
- Click, the thumbs-up. That feeling. Nobody asked what your title was. You were not frontend, not backend, not QA. You were someone who made it work.
- Hold that. We are going to come back to it.

## 2. The 1974 team (1:00)

**Thought:** a real team with a real idea, and the idea outlived the result.

- Netherlands, 1974 World Cup. Rinus Michels the coach, Johan Cruyff the conductor on the pitch.
- They lost the final to West Germany, 2 to 1. And still changed how football is played.
- The idea in one sentence: any player could take any position, because a teammate covered for him.

## 3. The line-up (1:30)

**Thought:** eleven specialists, each with a home position.

- This is the final. Jongbloed in goal. Suurbier, Rijsbergen, Haan, Krol at the back. Jansen, Neeskens, Van Hanegem in the middle. Rep, Cruyff, Rensenbrink up front.
- For those who do not follow football: every one of these is a specialist. A right back defends the right side. A striker scores. That was the whole game until this team.

## 4. Total Football (3:00), 7 clicks

**Thought:** you can go, because someone covers, and the shape never breaks.

- Click, the pass chain and the ball. From kick-off the Dutch kept the ball for about a minute. Cruyff was fouled in the box. Neeskens scored the penalty in the second minute before any German had touched the ball.
- Click, the plan for the first run. Red is the run, blue is the cover. Say the plan before the move each time: the board shows it first, then they go.
- Click, the run. Suurbier, the right back, overlaps up the wing and gets the ball. Jansen drops in to cover his place.
- Click, plan. Click, run. Neeskens, a midfielder, surges into the box and gets the next pass. Van Hanegem slides across to hold the middle.
- Click, plan. Click, run. Ball lost, and the same eleven drop into a compact block.
- Nobody is told to do this. You can go, because someone covers. The shape never breaks. That is Total Football.

## 5. Four positions (1:00)

**Thought:** the simplest picture of a team.

- Strip it down to four. Goalkeeper, defender, midfielder, striker.
- Four positions, four kinds of skill. We will put our own labels on this picture in a minute.

## 6 to 9. One player at a time (4 x 1:00)

**Thought:** each one is excellent at something and weak at something else.

- Goalkeeper. Brilliant with his hands, solid at the back, nobody expects him to score. Look at the attacking bar.
- Defender. Strong in the tackle, reads the game, decent pass out. He can score from a corner, but nobody builds the attack around him.
- Midfielder. Best passer on the pitch, runs all day, joins the attack, helps the defence. The widest profile of the four.
- Striker. One job, done brilliantly. Fast, clinical, not much use at the back. Every team needs one, and nobody asks him to keep goal.

## 10. Four together (0:45)

**Thought:** the team works because the shapes fit together.

- Four profiles. Every one has a red bar somewhere.
- Nobody here is complete. The team is.

## 11. The total footballer (1:30), 1 click

**Thought:** the total footballer is not a person, it is a team.

- Now imagine one player who could do all of it. Keep goal, defend, pass, score, run all day. All bars green. Let the room enjoy it.
- Click, the stamp. Does not exist. Not in 1974, not now.
- Michels never asked anyone to be this. He asked eleven specialists to be able to cover each other.
- Keep this in mind for the next part, because it is the same answer for us. And keep the stamp in mind for the demo at the end: we will find the one exception, in another sport.

## 12. Our four (1:30), 2 clicks

**Thought:** our lanes look exactly like the football positions.

- Click, the lanes. DevOps, QA, Backend, Frontend.
- Click, and they change one by one, with their card.
- DevOps is the goalkeeper: nobody notices him until something is down, and he is the only one allowed to touch production with his hands.
- QA is the defender: reads the game, catches what everyone else missed.
- Backend is the midfielder: the engine room, the one who passes.
- Frontend is the striker: the one who finishes, because the feature exists when someone can click it.
- Six axes now: frontend, backend, testing, operations, communication, judgment. Every card a different shape. That is a team.

## 13. AI assisted (2:00), 1 click

**Thought:** nobody became a total engineer. The gaps got smaller, and the first step into someone else's lane got cheap.

- Click, the gold bars and the lanes fade.
- The DevOps engineer can draft a React component. The frontend engineer can write the migration and read the logs. QA can write the fix, not only the bug report.
- Nobody became complete. Nobody needs to. What changed is the cost of the first step into somebody else's position.
- Two bars did not move much: communication and judgment. Those are still ours, and they matter most exactly when the tool is wrong.
- This is the answer from the stamp: not one super player, a team that can cover.

## 14. The whole team moves (1:00), 3 clicks

**Thought:** everybody moves, nobody is where they started, and it is still a shape.

- Eleven kits again. Click, the AI ring on each of them.
- Click, everyone moves. Watch: the goalkeeper stays, the rest are somewhere new. Still a shape, still covered.
- Click, and back. This is what the gold bars buy you. Not a super player, a team that can change shape without breaking.

## 15. The coach (2:30), 2 clicks

**Thought:** somebody decides the shape before the match. For a team of agents, that is you.

- Now the other job. Before the match, somebody decides the shape. Michels did it with a notebook.
- Click, the left page draws. The pitch, the crosses, the arrows. This one runs, that one covers, the ball goes there.
- Click, the right page draws. The same drawing for a team of agents. One plans, some build, one tests, some review, one ships. The red arrow: test fails, back to build.
- You are the coach of this team. And you still play. You still hold the ball at the end.

## 16. The ticket (1:00), 1 click

**Thought:** a normal story, and the first move is to split it, not to build it.

- Here is the match. One ticket. Export a report as PDF: company header, page numbers, the same filters as on screen. A week of work for one person.
- Click, the three subtasks. The first thing the orchestrator does is not building. It splits the story into three pieces that can be built at the same time: layout, endpoint, button.
- Now watch it go onto the pitch.

## 17. The run (3:00), 7 clicks

**Thought:** the agents play fast, but the ticket does not cross the line until you have held it.

Each click brings a note card next to the actors, so mostly read the pitch.

- Click. The orchestrator reads the ticket. It decides the shape, like the coach with the notebook. The coach on the touchline is you.
- Click. Three builders appear, the ticket splits into three scraps. Three pieces, in parallel.
- Click. A tester takes all three as they arrive.
- Click. Three reviewers. This is the point: not one, three, with three points of view. One reads the code, one thinks like an attacker, one thinks like the user.
- Click. Security pushes back. The scrap goes back to a builder: not good enough, go again.
- Click. Rebuilt, reviewed, ready to ship.
- Click. The ticket comes to the touchline, to you. Fast is fine. Unheld is not.

## 18. The docs (0:30)

**Thought:** this is a real feature, not a drawing.

- If anyone wants to read it afterwards: dynamic workflows in Claude Code. One script orchestrates the subagents you just watched.

## 19. The terminal, then live demo (5:00)

**Thought:** you describe the shape in one sentence, and the tool writes the file, runs the team, and changes this very presentation.

- This is what it looks like in real life. Not a pitch, a terminal.
- Switch to the terminal, Claude Code open in a copy of this repo. The prompt is one line, in `talk/DEMO.md`; the brief the agents read is `docs/sabonis.md`.
- The task: add one stop to this talk. Remember the stamp, "Does not exist"? Almost. Arvydas Sabonis. A 2.21 metre centre who shot three-pointers and passed like a guard. The closest thing to a total player, in another sport.
- Type the prompt: use a workflow for the task in the brief, a researcher, a builder, a checker, one pushback. Do not commit.
- Open `/workflows` and talk over the tree with the pitch. The researcher is refinement. The builder builds. The checker is the reviewer who can send it back.
- When it lands, open the poster from the copy and jump to the new stop. Sabonis is in the talk. The room just watched it happen.
- Ask Claude to show the workflow script it wrote. A JavaScript file, about 40 lines: phases, pipeline, the pushback branch. The notebook drawing as code, written for this one prompt.
- The point: you did not write the workflow and you did not open the editor. You drew the shape in a sentence. And it is still you who decides whether it gets merged.
- If it fails, say so and come back to the poster. The run stop shows the same thing.

## 20. The whole poster (1:00)

**Thought:** you did not need a label to make the first thing work. You do not need one now.

- Pull back. Where we started: the first program that worked. The 1974 team that changed the game by covering for each other. Our four, each excellent at something, each with a gold bar now. The notebook, the terminal.
- The pitch is the same pitch. What changed is that going into someone else's position is cheap, and somebody in the room has to draw the shape.
- Stay here for questions.
