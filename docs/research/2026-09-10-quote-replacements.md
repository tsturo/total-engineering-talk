# Replacements for the Kent Beck 2023 stop

All quotes checked against the primary page unless marked "unverified". Dates are publication dates.

## Candidates

### 1. Anthropic, "How AI Is Transforming Work at Anthropic" (2 Dec 2025)
Source: https://www.anthropic.com/research/how-ai-is-transforming-work-at-anthropic
Study: internal survey of 132 engineers and researchers, Aug 2025, plus 53 interviews and Claude Code usage data.
- Verbatim (engineer quoted): "becoming more full-stack... I can very capably work on front-end, or transactional databases, or API code, where previously I would've been scared to touch stuff I'm less of an expert on."
- Verbatim (backend engineer on building a complex UI): "I would not have been able to do it, definitely not on time."
- Figures: Claude used in 59% of work (28% a year earlier); self-estimated productivity +50% (was +20%); 27% of Claude-assisted work "would not have been done otherwise".
- Honest limit: report names a "paradox of supervision" (supervising Claude needs the very skills that may atrophy); more than half say they can fully delegate only 0-20% of their work.
- Serves the talk: range beyond one's label, first-hand, with numbers. Caveat: Anthropic studying Anthropic; say so on stage.

### 2. Thomas Dohmke, "Developers, Reinvented" (3 Aug 2025)
Source: https://ashtom.github.io/developers-reinvented and tweet https://x.com/ashtom/status/1952409910236291109
Study: 22 developers who use AI heavily.
- Attribution warning: "Either you have to embrace the AI, or you get out of your career" is a study participant's line. Dohmke then tweeted "The evidence is clear: Either you embrace AI, or get out of this career." Press attributes it to him directly; that is loose.
- Verbatim (participant): "Maybe we become less code producers and more code enablers. My next title might be Creative Director of Code."
- Verbatim (Dohmke): developers focus on "raising the ceiling of the work and outcomes we can accomplish".
- Serves the talk: ambition over efficiency. Weaknesses: n=22, vendor, and he left GitHub after this; "get out of this career" is a threat, not an invitation, which cuts against a Total Football tone.

### 3. Simon Willison (2025-2026)
- 19 Mar 2025, https://simonwillison.net/2025/Mar/19/vibe-coding/ : "If an LLM wrote the code for you, and you then reviewed it, tested it thoroughly and made sure you could explain how it works to someone else that's not vibe coding, it's software development."
- 7 Oct 2025, https://simonwillison.net/2025/Oct/7/vibe-engineering/ : "AI tools amplify existing expertise. The more skills and experience you have as a software engineer the faster and better the results you can get from working with LLMs and coding agents."
- 6 May 2026, https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/6/vibe-coding-and-agentic-engineering/ : "Those things have started to blur for me already, which is quite upsetting." and "if I haven't reviewed the code, is it really responsible for me to use this in production?"
- Serves the talk: the honest limit (review is the job; the AI does not know when it is wrong) and uncertainty made legitimate by a careful practitioner admitting his own line moved. Concrete: first-hand practice, no numbers.

### 4. Kent Beck, 2025-2026
- 25 Jun 2025, "Augmented Coding: Beyond the Vibes", https://newsletter.kentbeck.com/p/augmented-coding-beyond-the-vibes : built a B+ tree library in Rust (a language he was learning) and Python over four weeks. "I make more consequential programming decisions per hour, fewer boring vanilla decisions." On limits: "the genie was cheating, for example by disabling or deleting tests."
- 11 Jun 2025, Pragmatic Engineer podcast, https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/tdd-ai-agents-and-coding-with-kent : "languages don't matter anymore"; after 52 years of coding he has never had more fun; built a Smalltalk server and LSP he had long deferred.
- 1 Jul 2026, Pragmatic Engineer, https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/how-kent-beck-shapes-the-software : "Your ability to affect change in the world is gated by your ability to communicate with, to soothe, to understand other human beings." Calls this the "cosmic practical joke" of a coding career.
- Serves the talk: same author as the current stop, but 2025-2026, first-hand, and the "consequential decisions per hour" line is concrete. The 2026 line covers range (people skills, not only code). Note: Beck did not publicly retract the 90/10 post; the 2025 pieces are its continuation.

### 5. Andrej Karpathy
- 2 Feb 2025 tweet: "fully give in to the vibes, embrace exponentials, and forget that the code even exists"; he limited it to "throwaway weekend projects" and later called it "a shower of thoughts throwaway tweet" (https://x.com/karpathy/status/2019137879310836075).
- 17 Jun 2025, YC AI Startup School, transcript https://www.latent.space/p/s3 : "the hottest new programming language is English"; "keep the AI on the leash"; the "generation verification loop"; "jagged intelligence". He built an iOS app with no Swift, but the speedup vanished when MenuGen hit real infrastructure (auth, deploy, payments) - a good DevOps beat.
- Serves the talk: range (Swift without Swift) and the limit (leash, verification). Concrete: first-hand demo, no study.

### 6. Boris Cherny / Anthropic on Claude Code writing itself
- 27 Dec 2025, X, https://x.com/bcherny/status/2004897269674639461 : "In the last thirty days, 100% of my contributions to Claude Code were written by Claude Code".
- 29 Jan 2026, Fortune, https://fortune.com/2026/01/29/100-percent-of-code-at-anthropic-and-openai-is-now-ai-written-boris-cherny-roon/ : Anthropic spokesperson: ~90% of Claude Code is written by Claude Code; company-wide 70-90%.
- 11 Jun 2026, Fortune Brainstorm Tech, https://fortune.com/2026/06/11/anthropic-claude-boris-cherny-doesnt-write-code-by-hand-anymore/ : "I haven't written a line of code by hand in, I think, eight months now".
- Serves the talk: the ground moved, with a number. Weakness: vendor talking about his own product; use as colour, not as the poster note.

### 7. Sundar Pichai on Google code
- Oct 2024: "more than 25%" of new code (https://www.techradar.com/pro/alphabet-ceo-says-more-than-a-quarter-of-new-code-at-google-is-ai-generated).
- Fall 2025: 50%. 22 Apr 2026, Google Cloud Next: 75% of new code is AI-generated, reviewed by engineers (https://www.semafor.com/article/04/24/2026/google-ceo-says-75-of-companys-new-code-is-ai-generated, https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/infrastructure-and-cloud/google-cloud/cloud-next-2026-sundar-pichai/). I could not get his sentence verbatim from a fetchable page; present as a figure, not a quote.
- Caveat: "AI-generated" counts autocompleted characters; the number says little about who does what.

### 8. Steve Yegge, "Revenge of the Junior Developer" (22 Mar 2025)
Source: https://sourcegraph.com/blog/revenge-of-the-junior-developer (page blocked fetch; wording unverified).
- Thesis: developers become managers of agent fleets; juniors "less stuck in their ways" adapt faster. Contains dated timelines for agent fleets (2025-2026). Unusable as a quote: prediction-heavy, unverified wording, and the fart joke opening will not survive a corporate projector. His Oct 2025 book with Gene Kim ("Vibe Coding") is the cleaner reference if needed.

### 9. Martin Fowler / Thoughtworks
- 19 Nov 2025, Pragmatic Engineer, https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/martin-fowler : "the skills that make a great engineer remain largely unchanged"; frames LLMs as non-deterministic computing where engineers must ask "What are the tolerances of the non-determinism that we have to deal with?"
- May 2025, Thoughtworks Perspectives 36, https://www.thoughtworks.com/en-us/perspectives/edition36-ai-first-software-engineering/article : "AI-first approaches could completely change the way people think about programming".
- Apr 2026, Rachel Laycock (Thoughtworks CTO), https://www.thoughtworks.com/en-us/insights/cto-newsletter/edition1 : "Engineering rigor does not disappear when AI generates code. It relocates and becomes more important than ever."
- Serves the talk: legitimises uncertainty from a conservative source; Laycock's line is the best QA/DevOps-friendly sentence found.

### 10. Surveys with concrete figures
- Stack Overflow 2025 (49k respondents, released Dec 2025), https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/ai : 84% use or plan to use AI; 46% distrust its accuracy, 3% "highly trust"; top frustration, 66%: "AI solutions that are almost right, but not quite"; 45% say debugging AI code is time-consuming. Stack Overflow 2026 opened 23 Jun 2026, results not yet published.
- DORA 2025 (Sep 2025), https://dora.dev/research/2025/dora-report/ : ~90% adoption, median 2 hours a day, AI "is an amplifier"; adoption raises throughput and instability together.
- JetBrains 2025 (24,534 devs, Oct 2025), https://blog.jetbrains.com/research/2025/10/state-of-developer-ecosystem-2025/ : 85% use AI regularly; 68% expect AI proficiency to become a job requirement.
- METR RCT (Jul 2025), https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/ : 16 experienced maintainers, 246 issues, 19% slower with AI while believing they were 20% faster. The best "AI does not know when it is wrong, and neither do we" figure.
- No public survey found that asks "did AI let you work outside your specialty" with a percentage. The Anthropic internal study is the only concrete source for that claim.

## Aged badly, do not use
- Dario Amodei, 10 Mar 2025: AI writes "90% of code" in 3-6 months. Contested even by sympathetic observers (https://blog.redwoodresearch.org/p/is-90-of-code-at-anthropic-being).
- Yegge's agent-fleet timelines (2025-2026).
- Karpathy's Feb 2025 vibe coding tweet if presented as advice; he disowned the framing himself.
- Dohmke's "get out of this career" as his own words; it is a participant's line and reads as a threat.

## Top 5 for this talk
1. Anthropic engineer, "scared to touch stuff I'm less of an expert on" (Dec 2025). Exactly the talk's thesis, first-hand, with 132/59%/27% behind it.
2. Kent Beck, "more consequential programming decisions per hour, fewer boring vanilla decisions" (Jun 2025). Same respected voice as now, two years newer, concrete practice, and he learned Rust doing it.
3. Simon Willison, "reviewed it, tested it thoroughly and made sure you could explain how it works" (Mar 2025) paired with his May 2026 admission. Sets the review rule and shows the ground still moving.
4. METR, 19% slower while feeling 20% faster (Jul 2025). The honest limit as a controlled study, not an opinion.
5. Rachel Laycock, "Engineering rigor does not disappear when AI generates code. It relocates." (Apr 2026). Gives QA and DevOps their place in the story.
