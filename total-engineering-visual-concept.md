# Total Engineering: a presentation inside one poster

Visual concept for a 20–30 minute talk, with a 25-minute core.

This is a planning document. It describes the future presentation's composition, camera movement, and changing visual states. It does not produce the poster, animation, or presentation.

Companion: [talk structure and presenter script](total-engineering-talk.md). Keep that document's ten chapters and emotional arc, preceded by a brief opening title view. The concept below develops the earlier slide suggestions into one continuous visual space. Use this document for the visual direction and the companion for the spoken story.

## The central visual idea

**One large poster with several connected places. The football pitch is the central place for thinking about the team.**

The audience first sees the title: TOTAL FOOTBALL, with FOOTBALL struck through and ENGINEERING added by hand from the side. The camera then moves into a small thing someone made. Later, it pulls back to reveal the pitch. We return to that same pitch several times, seeing it divided, coordinated, and finally shared.

The important visual change happens in a familiar place: the internal FE, BE, QA, and DevOps boundaries fade while the players remain. They can move into new areas and support one another. Their identities stay recognizable throughout the transformation.

At the end, a wide view brings the first creation, the unfinished idea, and the team into the same composition. The audience can see where the story started and what it has grown to include.

The camera carries part of the argument:

- A close view makes the story personal.
- A view of several players makes a relationship visible.
- A view of the whole pitch makes shared responsibility visible.

Use “Prezi-like” to mean a continuous canvas with deliberate changes of scale. The concept does not depend on choosing Prezi or any particular production tool now.

### Does everything belong on the pitch?

No. The pitch explains how people work together. The personal stories have their own spaces and visual language.

| On the pitch | In their own places elsewhere on the poster |
| --- | --- |
| Roles and departmental boundaries | A first creation and the pleasure of making it work |
| Total Football and coordinated movement | Kent Beck's post and professional uncertainty |
| Total Engineering and broader contribution | A notebook containing an idea left unfinished |
| Team support and the assist | The audience's next idea and the actual people in the team |

Five chapters use the pitch directly: 2, 4, 5, 7, and 8. The other five use the human material. The closing wide view brings these places together.

The common paper, typography, and restrained palette make this one visual world. The first project can look like a first project, and the notebook can look like a notebook. They do not need football decorations. The surrounding spaces are editorial parts of the poster rather than literal areas of a stadium.

## What the poster looks like

Imagine a contemporary editorial poster about making things and working together. Its central football scene has the clarity of a coach's tactics board. The surrounding human material has a more personal, natural character.

The canvas is warm off-white. A deep green pitch occupies most of the central area. Its lines are simple and readable. Orange player markers provide the strongest color accents. Around the pitch sit a small first creation, an archival photograph, Beck's quotation, and an unfinished notebook sketch. A photograph of the real team belongs near the lower edge.

These elements belong to one spacious composition. They should feel placed on the same surface, with consistent alignment and generous empty space. Photographs can have a clean edge or disappear softly into the paper. Avoid a separate framed box around each chapter.

The master composition is approximately 16:9, with room around it for close views that need a little extra background. The pitch runs horizontally and retains recognizable football proportions. It should occupy roughly the middle three-fifths of the poster's width, leaving narrower areas on either side for the personal material.

### The fixed places

| Place | What lives there | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Upper centre, above the pitch | TOTAL FOOTBALL with FOOTBALL struck through and ENGINEERING handwritten beside it | The opening title and a recurring visual connection between the two ideas |

A small set of recurring objects gives the audience landmarks. Their positions stay fixed when the camera travels. Changing the camera view should not cause the notebook or photograph to appear in a different place.

## The opening title

**The title reads: TOTAL ~~FOOTBALL~~ ENGINEERING.**

This Markdown notation describes the wording. In the eventual artwork, the strike and added word should look like marks made by a person.

Set TOTAL and FOOTBALL in large, confident, near-black uppercase type on the warm paper. A two-line setting gives the lettering room: TOTAL above FOOTBALL. Cross only FOOTBALL with one quick, slightly irregular orange stroke. Keep the original word legible.

Add ENGINEERING in orange handwriting, entering from the lower right beside the crossed word. Let it overlap a little of the printed title's open space, like a thought added to an existing poster. Use broad, readable marker lettering, with a small natural slant and enough room for the entire word. The handwriting should be large enough to carry the new title at a glance.

TOTAL stays intact. FOOTBALL remains visible under the stroke, preserving the source of the idea. The added ENGINEERING makes the adaptation feel personal and intentional. Carry the same mark into the wider poster so the opening and later team transformation belong together.

### Suggested title sequence

1. Use the completed title as the holding view while the speaker gets ready. It should also work as a static cover.
2. If an animated entrance is wanted, begin with TOTAL FOOTBALL, draw the strike in about 0.4 seconds, then reveal the complete handwritten ENGINEERING in about 0.3–0.5 seconds. Play this once. Avoid a long letter-by-letter handwriting sequence.
3. Hold the completed title briefly. Then move away from its fixed location above the pitch and into the first creation at lower left. Keep the full pitch undisclosed until chapter 4.

The preferred version keeps the finished title visible from the start. The gesture already communicates the change without requiring an animation.

**Optional opening line:** “There's a Dutch idea behind this title. First, think back to the first thing you made work.”

Allow approximately 15 seconds for the title and transition, taken from the existing opening allocation. The ten substantive chapters still follow, and the core talk remains 25 minutes.

At chapter 5, bring this exact title back into view as the pitch becomes shared. The audience now understands the idea behind the handwritten change. Preserve the crossed word and handwriting rather than replacing them with a new plain title.

## The pitch

The pitch is the central visual for Totaalvoetbal and the recurring visual for the team. It should be recognizable immediately when first shown in full.

Use an overhead view. Include the touchlines, halfway line, centre circle, penalty areas, and goals. Keep the field flat and its orientation constant throughout the talk. Restrained texture can make it feel tactile without competing with the markers.

Use eleven simple numbered markers to establish a football team. They are illustrative players, not an exact historical lineup or a claim about the size of the development team. Keep the goalkeeper in the defensive area during the example. Show one team's movement without adding an opposing team that needs its own explanation.

The four engineering specialties are a temporary conceptual overlay. Do not assign frontend permanently to attack, QA to defence, or DevOps to the goalkeeper. That would reintroduce the fixed roles the story is questioning.

### The pitch has three main states

| State | What the audience sees | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Divided | Four broad areas labeled FE, BE, QA, and DevOps; markers remain within those areas; a ball stops at a boundary | Work encounters limits between specialties |
| Coordinated | The engineering overlay disappears; a short football movement demonstrates one player advancing while another covers | Flexible movement depends on a shared understanding |
| Shared | The same pitch and numbered players remain; a new contribution crosses a former boundary; support is visible; the opening title returns to view | A common engineer identity can include different strengths and broader contributions |

The ordinary football markings remain in every state. The temporary departmental boundaries have a visibly different style, such as fine charcoal dividers and translucent area shading.

Keep the divided state calm and matter-of-fact. Its purpose is to make boundaries visible. The shared state gains energy through movement and open space, with no change in the number of people.

### How to show movement and cover

Use one short, deliberately schematic action:

1. Pause on a stable arrangement. Emphasize two outfield players and the ball. Other markers remain present at lower emphasis.
2. An outfield player moves forward into available space.
3. A teammate adjusts into the space that needs covering. A short trail helps the audience see what changed.
4. Hold the new arrangement so the presenter can explain the relationship.

Treat the movement and the covering adjustment as one coordinated action. Do not let the first player run far ahead while the rest of the team remains frozen for several seconds.

This is an explanatory diagram inspired by the concept of Total Football. It is not a reconstruction of a specific match. The history and attribution notes in the companion document remain the reference for factual claims.

## How AI appears

Keep AI's visual presence modest. The audience should see people gaining ways to explore and contribute.

During chapter 5, a few faint dotted paths can briefly appear around the highlighted engineer's marker. They represent possible approaches to explore. One path becomes solid when the engineer chooses a small next step, and a teammate moves to support it. The unused possibilities fade.

Use a small label, “AI assistance,” only while explaining that moment. Dotted paths represent possibilities, not verified solutions or guaranteed capability. The human contribution and the support around it remain the focus.

The same player markers remain before and after AI appears. Avoid robot players, AI mascots, or disappearing teammates. The central transformation is the team's expanded range.

## The camera journey

Use three broad distances:

- **Close:** One artifact, quotation, or relationship fills the frame. Roughly three to five times closer than the full-poster view, depending on legibility.
- **Pitch:** Most or all of the field is visible. Enough space remains to understand movement and cover.
- **Poster:** The whole composition is visible, with the pitch as its strongest element.

These are proposed framing ranges. The finished design should choose the actual crops by what remains readable on a projector.

The opening does not show a tiny overview of the whole talk. Begin with a close view of the altered title, then move into the first creation. The first complete pitch reveal belongs to Totaalvoetbal. The complete poster gets its clearest, fullest presentation at the end.

## Chapter-by-chapter art direction

### 1. IT WORKS

**The image:** A small creation with a visible result. A true first project from the presenter would work especially well. A simple page, an early game, or a completed automation can carry the feeling. Choose one object rather than a collage of every possible beginning.

**The view:** Close enough that the audience has not yet discovered the football pitch. Some warm paper or empty space at the edge hints that this object belongs to a larger world.

**The reveal:** The meaningful result appears first. Then IT WORKS. The timing should recall the moment of satisfaction after something finally succeeds. Once that moment has happened, hold the frame through the reflection.

**Presenter cue:** “Can you remember the first time you made a computer do something and thought: it works?”

**Important detail:** Keep this artifact in the same location until the end. It becomes a visual memory the camera can return to.

### 2. OUR FORMATION

**The image:** A close crop of the pitch's middle, with four temporary areas. Show enough for all four labels to read, while keeping the goals and most recognizable outer markings outside the crop. This foreshadows the pitch without spending the full reveal yet.

**The reveal:** Introduce the role labels one at a time. Move the ball between a couple of areas, with a short pause at a divider. One repeated stop is sufficient to communicate the handoff.

**Presenter cue:** “Sometimes the moment we celebrate becomes ‘my part is done.’”

**The feeling:** Recognizable friction. The viewer should see competent people inside a constrained arrangement.

### 3. RECALIBRATION

**The image:** Beck's quotation sits in a permanent position on the upper-right side of the poster. The preferred close view contains “I need to recalibrate.” in large type, plus his name and 18 April 2023. The supplied post may appear first as a brief source view.

**The reveal:** If the full post is shown, give people enough time to read it. Then reduce its emphasis and bring the final sentence forward. Keep both sides of his observation available so the sequence preserves his meaning.

**Presenter cue:** “The sentence I want us to hold onto is the last one.”

**The feeling:** A thoughtful pause. The motion here should be minimal. The accompanying script explains that the numbers are a personal reaction, with the qualifications documented in the talk file.

### 4. TOTAALVOETBAL

**The image:** The complete football pitch appears for the first time. The archival photograph to its upper left becomes visible as a supporting historical reference. The close quotation recedes to its place on the wider canvas.

**The reveal:** Introduce TOTAALVOETBAL above or beside the pitch. Clear the engineering dividers before starting the football example. Emphasize two players and demonstrate movement with cover. Then hold the new arrangement.

**Presenter cue:** “I can move because we understand what needs to remain covered.”

**The feeling:** Recognition and possibility. This is the first major change of scale, and the point at which the audience understands why the talk lives on a pitch.

**Photograph treatment:** Keep the actual historical photo separate from the illustrative tactical movement. Avoid implying that the markers reproduce the pictured moment. A short historical caption becomes readable only when needed.

### 5. TOTAL ENGINEERING

**The image:** The camera remains in the place the audience has just understood. Retain the numbered markers and field orientation. A brief reminder of the former departmental dividers may appear as faint outlines, then disappear.

**The reveal:** A few possible routes appear near one marker. One becomes the chosen contribution, supported by another marker. Widen slightly to bring the opening title back into view: TOTAL, crossed-through FOOTBALL, and handwritten ENGINEERING. The whole team remains visible. Keep the title in its completed state rather than replaying the pen stroke.

**Presenter cue:** “FE, BE, QA, and DevOps describe capabilities we bring. Engineer describes a responsibility we share.”

**The feeling:** The central idea becomes visible. Something familiar has gained more freedom.

**This is the signature transition:** Keep it short enough to understand in one viewing, then leave the resulting composition still while the presenter develops the argument.

### 6. THE IDEA YOU PARKED

**The image:** A close view of a notebook sketch in the lower-right area. It suggests an idea the author understood partly but never completed. The drawing is simple and deliberately unfinished.

**The reveal:** As the imagined Monday-morning story develops, one missing connection becomes clear. A small result or a photograph suggesting the person helped can appear within the same local composition. This is a visual metaphor for trying an idea, not a simulated product demonstration.

**Presenter cue:** “There is our opening feeling again: it works.”

**The feeling:** Quiet excitement. Leave enough empty space for people to imagine their own parked idea. A tiny orange mark can connect this scene visually with the first creation, without turning the software artifact into a literal football.

### 7. TEAM SHAPE

**The image:** Return to the pitch in the shared state established in chapter 5. The previous contribution and its support remain in their last positions.

**The reveal:** Make the supporting players easier to notice. Reveal Trust and Practice near the action. A small training photograph can replace the archive photograph at its existing location if needed, but the main pitch view should remain dominant.

**Presenter cue:** “I haven't done this before.” Pause. “Let's look at it together.”

**The feeling:** Permission to learn, backed by other people. Avoid turning the moment into a checklist of engineering controls. Those details can stay in the spoken explanation.

### 8. THE ASSIST

**The image:** A closer crop of two adjacent players from the same arrangement. Show enough surrounding space to understand their relationship.

**The reveal:** A short passing line appears once. Then emphasize the assisting player while leaving the receiver visible. Do not turn the scorer into an isolated hero portrait.

**Presenter cue:** “Think of someone whose help made you a better engineer.”

**The feeling:** Recognition of other people. Hold the frame during the pause so a real colleague's name can come to mind. A true team anecdote could fit here without adding another visual destination.

### 9. OUR NEXT SPRINT

**The image:** Return to the same notebook. It retains the progress seen in chapter 6. An open area on the page leaves room for the audience to imagine their own next idea. Keep this scene in the ordinary visual language of making something.

**The reveal:** One idea. A teammate. Keep the full sentence-completion prompt in the script, unless rehearsal shows a short visible version is necessary for the exercise.

**Presenter cue:** “I have wanted to build or improve ___, and my next small step could be ___.”

**The feeling:** A possible next action. Freeze all animation during the 45–60 second audience reflection and the short volunteered response. The frame should feel settled.

### 10. ENGINEERS

**The image:** First, a close or medium view of the actual team photograph near the bottom of the poster. Then a slow pullback reveals the entire composition, with the shared pitch at its centre.

**The reveal:** Briefly emphasize the first creation in its original location when the presenter recalls it. Restore emphasis to the whole team. The major closing text is ENGINEERS, with “What shall we build together?” as the final line. The altered opening title remains smaller above the pitch, with its original strike and handwriting intact.

**Presenter cues:** “Your specialty is your home position.” Hold. “The whole product is your pitch.” Widen to the final view.

**The feeling:** A personal beginning has become a shared possibility. The people, their experience, and the ideas all belong in the same picture.

**The final composition:** Keep the pitch and team most prominent. Other artifacts are recognizable landmarks. Their detailed captions and local chapter titles are hidden. Hold this view for questions.

## What stays, what changes, and what disappears

The poster needs a few distinct layers so every zoom is a readable composition.

| Layer | Behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Ground | Paper, pitch, and ordinary field markings stay fixed |
| Master title | Printed TOTAL FOOTBALL, its strike, and handwritten ENGINEERING share one fixed location and retain their completed appearance after the opening |
| Landmarks | The first creation, archive, quotation area, notebook, and team photo have fixed positions; their visibility can change |
| Team state | Numbered markers retain their identities and the positions reached by completed actions |
| Temporary explanation | Department dividers, movement trails, support emphasis, and AI possibilities appear only while explaining them |
| Local text | A chapter title or short labels appear near the current subject and disappear when leaving it |
| Closing text | ENGINEERS and the final question appear for the final view |

“Hidden” should mean genuinely absent from that viewing state. Do not leave tiny, unreadable paragraphs scattered across the overview. Objects can remain as visual landmarks while their detailed words disappear.

Returning to a location should preserve the story's progress. The notebook should not become unfinished again after its reveal. The pitch should not silently return to departmental divisions after the shared state has been introduced.

## Motion direction

Use movement when it changes what the audience can understand. Most of the talk should use stationary views.

As starting points for later rehearsal:

- Small emphasis or opacity changes: roughly 0.3–0.5 seconds.
- Short moves between neighboring views: roughly 0.8–1.2 seconds.
- The first full-pitch reveal and final pullback: roughly 1.5–2 seconds.
- A football movement or pass: roughly 1–1.5 seconds, followed by a still frame.

For distant destinations, widen enough to establish direction, travel, then settle into the next close view. Avoid a long high-speed journey over enlarged text. Allow only one attention-demanding change at a time: settle the camera before asking people to follow player movement or read a quotation.

Keep the horizon and text upright. Use no spins, tumbling words, bounce, or endless motion. Do not zoom deep into the hole of a letter or transform a person into an abstract symbol. Smooth, restrained movement suits the reflective parts of this talk.

The presenter advances each important reveal. There should be enough time to explain it, pause, or go back. Replaying a prepared view should restore its complete visual state rather than depending on a long chain of previous clicks.

For a future version with reduced movement, the same compositions can use cuts or short dissolves. A static PDF can show the meaningful stopped views in order, with the final poster as the closing page. Both are later production options.

## Color, typography, and photography

| Element | Proposed treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Canvas | Warm paper, approximately #F4F1E8 |
| Pitch | Deep green, approximately #173F35, with restrained texture |
| Field lines | Soft white with enough contrast at the full-pitch view |
| Players and key emphasis | Netherlands orange, approximately #F36C21 |
| Text on paper | Near-black, approximately #151515 |
| Photography | Authentic archival monochrome paired with natural contemporary team images |
| Main titles | Bold condensed sans serif, upright and plainly set; the added ENGINEERING in the master title uses large orange marker handwriting |
| Small labels and attribution | A clear companion sans serif |

Orange supplies the strongest accents. Avoid making every annotation orange. Keep the visual distinction between players, the ball, and movement paths readable through shape and line style as well as color.

Judge type at each final camera stop. A label that is readable in a close view can disappear in the overview. It should not remain as a distracting speck of text. Plan room for the Beck quotation so it can be read without a continuous zoom.

Photos belong to specific moments in the story. The same first creation and notebook recur intentionally as callbacks. Reusing them helps the audience recognize the place and what changed there.

## The three moments to spend the most design effort on

1. **The pitch reveal:** The earlier crop suddenly belongs to a whole field. It makes the Total Football metaphor concrete.
2. **The shared pitch:** Boundaries disappear while the same people remain and coordinate. It makes the proposed change in team identity visible.
3. **The final pullback:** The audience sees the first creation, parked idea, and present team as parts of one composition. It completes the emotional story.

If those moments work, the other camera moves can be very simple.

## Later production handoff

The eventual designer needs one master composition, one opening title stop, ten chapter stops, and a small number of reveal states inside them. Use a few deliberate reveals per chapter. Avoid adding movement just because the tool makes it possible.

Materials to choose later:

- The printed title, a single hand-drawn strike, and readable orange ENGINEERING lettering.
- One true first-project image, or a clearly illustrative substitute.
- One verified Dutch football archive image, using the sources already collected in the talk document.
- The supplied Beck post or a clean, attributed quotation.
- One notebook sketch for the parked idea.
- One natural photograph of the real team, if available.
- A consistent set of pitch markings, player markers, and movement lines.

Keep the source links and historical qualifications from the talk document in the production notes. This visual concept adds no new historical claims or AI performance statistics.

Before production is considered complete, check that every camera stop reads from the back of a room, all movement supports the spoken cue, and a return to an earlier location restores a coherent state. The final wide view should also work as a standalone poster: a clear title, a recognizable pitch, and a few meaningful human details.
